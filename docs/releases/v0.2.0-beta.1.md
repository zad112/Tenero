**Tenero v0.2.0-beta.1: the fifth test release, and the first on a new network. An experiment, not a product.**

> **Unaudited. One developer. Nothing on this network has any value. The network can be reset at any time, and your coins on it will be gone when it is.**
> Do not put anything on it that you cannot lose. A stop-mining notice, if one is ever needed, is posted only in the pinned issue:
> https://github.com/zad112/Tenero/issues/1 (only the owner can post there; anything claiming to be a notice anywhere else is not from the project).

> **This is a TEST release. "Released" here means "published for people to try": it does not mean finished, reviewed, audited or safe.** Still in testing, and the author expects to reset the network and to find bugs.

## The big change: a hard fork, and a new network called `beta`

**Nodes of v0.1.0-alpha.4 and earlier cannot follow the `beta` network, and a node of this version on `beta` is on a different chain from everything before it.** The `beta` network has a fresh genesis (label "tenero beta network 1", chain id
`577cc63dfdf445eb26b712fa422b95a082ddcac2249e34de9489cb87be23641b`), no premine (the genesis creates no coin; a test checks this on the real store), the same real proof of work and the same starting difficulty as `alpha`, and its own ports (control 38342, peers 38343). **Your `alpha` coins stay on `alpha`; they cannot be moved to `beta`.** A wallet made on `alpha` can be restored on `beta` from its 24 words (it starts empty there).

* **What the fork changes: a transaction is limited by its size, not by a count of inputs.** Before, a transaction could have at most 32 inputs, and a payment that needed more said "more than 32 outputs". Now a transaction may take at most **75,000 bytes** (half of the block-size floor), and there is no limit on the number of inputs besides that: **95 fit in a payment to one person, 93 in one with 16 outputs** (measured with real proofs). Outputs stay at 16. The proof field was raised from 32 KiB to 64 KiB, which a transaction of that many inputs needs. Every input costs about 772 bytes and its share of the fee.
* **The `alpha` network stays for a short time, for people who want to move over.** This version still runs on `alpha`, and there it judges transactions by the old limits (32 inputs, 32 KiB of proof), so it agrees with the alpha.4 nodes that are still on it. **The `alpha` seed stays up only for a day or so; after that `alpha` may have no node to start from.**
* **The program has one `beta` seed, built in: the author's second server, `195.26.244.245:38343`.** One computer, one person, the same caveats as the `alpha` seed.
* **Node and app must be the same version.** The control interface (between the node and the wallet, the miner and the app) gained a field and a request, so an older program cannot talk to this node and the reverse. **Upgrade them together.**

## What else is new

* **Payments are split for you.** A payment to more than 15 people, or that needs more pieces (separate amounts you were paid) than one transaction can carry, is made as **several transactions that spend different pieces**; one person's large payment arrives as several amounts that add up. What cannot be built at once (a transaction's change can be spent only after 10 blocks) is reported, not lost. **`tenero-wallet pay-many --file`** reads `ADDRESS AMOUNT` lines.
* **Combining pieces.** If you were paid many small amounts (block rewards, say), the wallet app's Send tab has **Combine pieces** and the command line has `sweep` and `combine --pieces N`: they make many pieces into fewer, larger ones, show a summary and the fee first, and send nothing until you say so. The new piece can be spent after about 10 blocks.
* **Mining pools, a first version.** **`tenero-pool`** is a pool; **`tenero-miner --pool`** and the app's **Mining tab ("On a pool")** work for one **with no node at all** (a node is still needed to see and spend what the pool pays you). **A pool keeps the block rewards and pays you by its own rules: nothing in the protocol or the chain makes it pay.** The pool also sees your address and your internet address. The pool's public key is pinned by the miner, so a person between you and the pool is refused. **`tenero-poolcheck`** checks any pool against the written protocol (`docs/POOL_PROTOCOL.md`).
  * **One pool is built in: the author's test pool on `beta`, `195.26.244.245:38335`** (the app's pool field, left empty, and `tenero-miner --pool default`). **It is one computer run by one person.** It is **PPLNS** (the reward of a block is shared by the work of the newest shares, whatever rounds they fell in), pays once an hour every miner owed at least 0.1 coins, takes no fee, and pays the network's fees itself. **The app still mines alone unless you choose a pool.**
  * **What was measured:** a seed, the pool's own node and the pool ran together on the author's rented server (11 GiB), with a GPU miner working for the pool: the two nodes held 4.0 GiB each (4,200,768 and 4,200,660 KiB resident), the pool process was small, and 3.2 GiB stayed available. **The pool found blocks and counted shares for a real GPU on the real `beta` chain.**
  * **A payout was seen (one):** on 2026-10-07 at 09:01 UTC the pool paid its one miner (the author) 219.74999999 coins in one transaction (fee about 0.031 coins), and the miner's wallet showed it as received at block 79. The pool's own line then said "1 still to pay" (the wallet could not build every part of the payment in that round; the pool tries again after 10 minutes), and **ten minutes later, at 09:11 UTC, the pool logged a second payment of 18.025000001 coins (fee about 0.029), "0 still to pay"**; the two add up to exactly 400 coins. **Only the first payment was checked in the miner's wallet**; the second is the pool's own report. It was the author's own miner and own wallet, on the author's own pool.
  * **What was NOT seen:** more than one miner; a payout to anyone but the author; the pool under load; the real proof of work against `tenero-poolcheck` (two of its checks need a valid share and are skipped there); the first minute's 3 refused shares have no confirmed cause. Job declaration (a miner building its own blocks) is not built.
* **"Pieces", not "coins" or "outputs".** The wallet's own words changed: your balance is made of separate **pieces**, one for each payment you received, and TNR is only what they are worth.
* **The miner screen says "pool" and not "node"** when it works for a pool, and the app no longer stops a pool miner when no node is running (it stopped every miner then, found by a test).

## What is this

The Rust program for the **`beta` network** (and, for a short time, `alpha`). Rules: `docs/CONSENSUS_V2.md` 6.2 (the size limit), `docs/POOL_PROTOCOL.md` and
`docs/RUNNING_A_POOL.md` (pools), all in the repository.

## What is in the download

`tenerod` (the node), `tenero-miner`, `tenero-wallet` (command line), `tenero-wallet-gui` (the wallet app: a window that also starts and stops the node and the miner), `tenero-seedcheck`, **`tenero-pool`
and `tenero-poolcheck`**, the licence, `THIRD-PARTY-LICENCES.txt`, a README, `RUNNING.md` (how to run it) and `RUNNING_A_POOL.md` (how to run a pool). Every program prints which build it is with `--version` (the app shows it in its About tab).

## Check the download before you run it

The files are **not code-signed**, so Windows SmartScreen (and some antivirus programs) will warn about them, as they do for most small unsigned programs. That
warning is not a verdict on the file; the checksum is how you check it is the file published here.

**Your antivirus may also flag these programs as a "coin miner"** (labels vary: "coin miner", "PUA", "potentially unwanted application", sometimes "trojan"). **That is partly true: `tenero-miner` is a miner**, and the wallet app and the node can start it; it runs your GPU or CPU
at full load, which is what those detectors look for. **Nothing mines unless you start it** (the Start button on the app's Mining tab, the `tenero-miner` program, or the node's `mine` setting, off by default). The author has not tested the programs against any named antivirus. Check the
checksum, or build from source; if you decide to trust the download, exclude only its own folder, never the whole drive, and do not switch your protection off. Download only from this page: real malware is often hidden in "free miner" downloads from elsewhere.

Windows (PowerShell):

    Get-FileHash .\tenero-0.2.0-beta.1-windows-x64.zip -Algorithm SHA256

Linux:

    sha256sum -c SHA256SUMS --ignore-missing

The result must equal the line for your file in `SHA256SUMS` on this page. The packages are built by a public GitHub Actions workflow from the tagged commit
(`release.yml`); **they are not verified to be bit-for-bit reproducible.**

## What you need

* **To run a node, the wallet, or the CPU miner:** nothing else, except memory: **a node needs about 4.3 GiB of free RAM** (it holds one 4 GiB proof-of-work dataset; 4.2 GB resident was read for each of two nodes on the author's Linux server on `beta`, 2026-10-07). At the first block of each 100-block epoch it pauses while it builds the next dataset (about 3 seconds on a fast PC). Mining adds its own memory on top.
* **To mine on a GPU:** an NVIDIA GPU with a current driver **and the CUDA Toolkit 13.x installed** (the miner uses NVIDIA's NVRTC and cuBLASLt libraries, which come with the Toolkit, not with the driver; nothing from NVIDIA is bundled here). Without them the miner says so and does not start on the GPU. **Only a 16 GB card has been tried.** Mining for a pool needs the same card and **no node**.
* **CPU mining on `beta` is impractical** (the starting difficulty is the same as `alpha`'s). Plan on a GPU.
* **GPU test status of this build: tested by the author on one machine, 2026-10-07, at commit `e727550`** (four commits before the tagged one; the commits in between changed only documentation (the README, `docs/RUNNING_A_POOL.md` and these notes) and no program code, which `git diff e727550 v0.2.0-beta.1 --stat` shows; the tests were run from a clean checkout of `e727550`, with no miner running): NVIDIA GeForce RTX 5070 Ti, driver 617.14, CUDA Toolkit 13.4. The GPU engine's 11 checks against the CPU passed (`cargo test --release -p tenero-gpu -- --ignored --test-threads=1`). The end-to-end check passed: the GPU mined 20 blocks with the real parameters (100 blocks to a dataset, batch 128) and a CPU node verified every one in full. The speed test read **32,323, 33,646, 35,472 and 36,061 attempts a second at batches of 32, 64, 128 and 256** (15 s each, one run, one card: an example, not a guarantee).
* **To run a pool beside a seed:** `docs/RUNNING_A_POOL.md`. The pool holds no dataset of its own (its node checks the shares), so a seed, the pool's node and the pool need about 8.2 GiB of dataset, measured as above.

## Honest limits

* **Linux:** the **node**, **`tenero-wallet`**, **`tenero-pool`** and **`tenero-poolcheck`** were built in WSL2 (Ubuntu 26.04) and run on a rented Linux server (the author's, 2026-10-07). **The node (twice: the seed and the pool's own node) and the pool ran as systemd services**, a GPU miner on a Windows PC worked for the pool; **`tenero-wallet` (it made the pool's wallet) and `tenero-poolcheck` (it passed) were run by hand there.** CI builds and tests the Linux programs. **Nothing else has run on Linux:** the Linux wallet app has not been opened on any desktop, and the Linux GPU miner has not run. The glibc floor is copied from the release workflow's log (a dry run, 2026-10-07, of the build at commit `0e682db`): **the node, the miner, the command-line wallet, the seed checker, the pool and the pool checker need glibc 2.34 or newer; the wallet app needs 2.35 or newer.** That is what the programs ask for, **measured by the workflow, not tried on an old system**; Ubuntu 22.04 has glibc 2.35.
* **The built-in pool reaches the network only through the `beta` seed, in practice.** Its node listens (port 38336; a fresh node on another computer, given only that address, synced the chain from it, 2026-10-07), but a node never dials a second port on an IP it is already connected to, so nodes that hold the seed do not also connect to the pool's node, and the seed is the only other node on the network, so it has one peer (read on the author's server, 2026-10-07, after it began to listen: `peers 1 (in 0, out 1)`; no inbound peer seen). If the seed is down and no other node has connected, the blocks the pool finds reach nobody and are dropped when it reconnects. Seed and pool are one person's server, so they fail together. **The pool's wallet is on that server, and its node is now a public, unaudited target:** the wallet's files are not yet hidden from the node by a sandbox rule (planned, not done as of this note).
* **Known flaw: the programs create their secret key files readable by every user of the machine** (the pool's `pool.key`, the node's `node.key`; the node's `control.cookie` too), because they are written with the default file mode. What protects them is the mode of the folder that holds them (`700`), which `docs/RUNNING_A_POOL.md` tells a pool operator to set and to check; found on the author's server, 2026-10-07, and not fixed in this release.
* **The pool is the part of this project that has run least.** One miner, one run, one server. See "What was NOT seen" above. **A pool keeps the block rewards and pays by its own rules**, holds a wallet on a public server, knows every miner's address and internet address, and is one person's computer.
* **There is one seed per network, built in, and both are the author's.** If one is down, a brand-new node on that network has nowhere to start (one that has run before remembers its peers), and its operator could show a new node a false chain. You can add your own seeds (`--seed ip:port`, or the wallet app's Settings) or drop the built-in one (`no_builtin_seeds`).
* **"Let other nodes connect to me"** (a tick box in the wallet app's Settings, off by default) needs a TCP port forwarded on your router and allowed in your firewall, and it lets strangers connect to your computer, with software that is unaudited. See `docs/RUNNING.md`.
* **The wallet uses an interim output scheme that is not Carrot**; it does not give Monero-style privacy. **Splitting a payment or combining pieces links those pieces to one wallet for anyone who reads the chain**, as any transaction with several inputs does. The seed phrase, passphrase file and message signatures are unaudited.
* **A payment proof for a transaction that pays several people covers the first person only.**
* **The verification cost of a transaction with 95 inputs has not been measured as a denial-of-service risk** (`docs/CONSENSUS_V2.md` 6.2 says so).
* **The proof of work** is designed to be hard to build a special chip for; that is an argument, not a measurement, and no one outside the project has reviewed it.
* Fuzzing has run for hours on six targets without a crash (on the code of earlier releases); that shows no bug was found in that time, not that none exists.
* Known flaws are listed in `docs/KNOWN_ISSUES.md` and `docs/THREAT_MODEL.md` in the repository, including the ones nobody has fixed.
